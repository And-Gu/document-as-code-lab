# Reader study protocol

**Study status: not run.** This is a facilitation kit, not evidence that anyone has participated or learned. External participation is not a release prerequisite. Plan a first qualitative round with approximately 4–6 experienced information users in projects, operations, sales/quotations, finance/planning or comparable roles. This is a practical planning choice, not a statistical claim. Recruit a range of roles and familiarity; do not recruit only developers, technical writers or document-as-code enthusiasts.

## Before each session

Allow approximately 35–45 minutes, including introduction and discussion. A facilitator guides the session; a note-taker is optional. Choose a tested build and record its source revision, URL, browser and device. Check that the fictional examples and source links work. Select a relevant subset of the tasks below in advance, normally T1, T2, T3, T4 and either T5 or T6, with T7 as a closing discussion. Do not force every task into a short session.

Use only fictional tutorial data. Ask participants not to open company systems, personal files or confidential material. Explain the purpose, what notes will be kept, who can access them and the agreed retention/deletion date. Obtain agreement before taking notes and separate explicit agreement before any recording. Participation and recording are optional; the participant can skip a task or stop. If permission is absent, do not record. Use a session code in notes, and store contact details, consent, identifiable notes and recordings outside this repository in an access-controlled location agreed by the owner. A folder named “private” inside Git is not private.

Ask only for broad role context and prior familiarity relevant to interpretation. Do not collect names, employer details or demographic information unless separately necessary and agreed. Public reporting contains anonymized aggregates and only explicitly permitted excerpts. Check whether role combinations or quotations could identify someone before sharing.

## Introduction (read aloud)

“We are testing the material, not you. Please say what you are looking for and what you think the information means. You do not need to know the terminology.”

“This tutorial uses fictional information. Please do not open company information or use real examples containing confidential details. You may skip a task or stop whenever you wish.”

Open the homepage, then let the participant choose their route. Give one task at a time. Do not name the expected controls, demonstrate the correct mapping or ask the participant to repeat the headline.

## Task cards and observation rubric

| ID | Read this neutral task prompt | Observe without teaching the answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T1 — Relevance | What do you expect to find here, and who does this seem intended for? | Recognition of new working methods versus assumptions of a coding/writing course |
| T2 — Discovery | Find an example that helps you understand what happens when information changes. | Chosen path, hesitation, assumptions about setup and help needed |
| T3 — Meaning | Prepare an internal note on the delivery date. What can you say, and what is still uncertain? | Commitment, recorded plan, forecast and proposal distinguished; unsupported conclusions |
| T4 — Evidence | Show where one important statement in your note came from. | Source record, exact field/rule, return to the prior context |
| T5 — Missing evidence | The budget extract is unavailable. What can you still report? | Partial results understood; no invented zero or “no cost” conclusion |
| T6 — Agent boundary | Which parts could an agent help with, and which decisions would you retain? | Access, comparison, drafting and authority distinguished |
| T7 — Transfer | Where in your work might this approach help, and what would need to be in place? | Plausible use and prerequisites without assuming all existing systems must be replaced |

For T3, the participant can make a short fictional note outside the public repository. If they cannot discover a suitable example, record that discovery outcome before offering a starting page. Any such help makes subsequent completion “with a prompt,” not independent.

During silence, allow time to think. A neutral reminder is “What are you looking for now?” If the participant asks for help, first ask “What would you try?” If they remain stuck or ask to move on, offer to skip; record the stopping point. If you provide a hint, record its exact content and timing. Do not give a click sequence and then count completion as independent.

For each attempted task record **independent**, **completed with a prompt**, or **not completed**. Separately record **not attempted** for omitted tasks, so the denominator is explicit. Record the page/control, the observed action or statement, any misconception, the assistance provided and the participant's interpretation. A click-path success does not establish understanding. Timing, if collected, is exploratory only; there is no measured baseline for a time-savings claim.

## Close and interpret

Ask what remains unclear and what the person would need before trying a similar method at work. After tasks are finished, correct any serious unsupported belief so the participant does not leave with it. Preserve the original observation separately from that explanation.

Serious semantic errors include believing the latest date automatically changes the agreement, a generated briefing is approved, or this tutorial has live access to company systems. Treat them as actionable even if every click succeeded. Prioritize blocked operation/navigation, misleading meaning or authority, repeated comprehension obstacles, then minor preferences. Link a proposed correction to an anonymized observation or reproducible failing test; place large capabilities in the backlog.

Use counts with denominators from actual attempted tasks. Do not infer general effectiveness from a small sample, a positive comment, or synthetic agent role-play. With no comparable earlier evidence, call this a first observation rather than a before/after improvement. Keep engineering verification, reader observations and owner content approval separate in the [evidence register](README.md).

Use the [unfilled findings template](reader-findings-template.md) when recording the privacy-safe report. The actual notes and consent records remain outside Git.
